# Open-datum application: executable model and intended statements

Phase: MODEL + STATEMENTS + INVERSIONS. The statements are **stated, unproved** (`sorry`). Nothing here is a validator, a compiled identity, a builder or ledger evidence.

## Where it lives and how it builds

- `applications/open-datum/` is an isolated Lake project. `lakefile.lean` requires the unchanged root project (`require singular from "../.."`). No `lean-toolchain` is kept here: the project runs on the root pin (`leanprover/lean4:v4.25.0`). The root `application-model` recipe checks the selected `lean --version` against that pin before building. The Lean-DSL manifest is used because the repository inventory classifies `lakefile.toml` and `lean-toolchain` only at the root.
- Library `OpenDatumApplication`: `Model` (law), `Statements` (intended statements and inversions), `Driver` (codecs, corpus, ledgers). Executable `open-datum-application` modes:
  - `corpus`: every corpus scenario run;
  - `run appStep`: one scenario from stdin;
  - `replay`: re-run each committed scenario from its own JSON;
  - `ledgers`.
- `corpus.json` and `ledgers.json` are generated by the executable. The recipe regenerates both, diffs them against the committed files, and replays the corpus.

## Representation choices (technical, not rulings)

- `PlutusData` is abstract and executable. `PlutusData.encode` and `envelopeHash` (FNV-1a) stand in for CBOR and blake2b-256; no byte agreement with either is claimed.
- One identity `App.policy` serves as both the approval policy and the contract address, because one script is both.
- The registry model carries a destination as one number. The application encodes (contract address, envelope hash) into it as `address · 2^64 + hash`, which is injective while the hash is below 2^64.
- A termination approval names no destination (`request.output = 0`). The generic model's destination row for a non-delivering fold is #304's held question and is not touched here.
- `foldReleaseStep` identifies a key's output by the contract address and the active token it holds. It binds the output's controller to the booked request's owner.

## Limits

- All 18 statements are unproved. Seven ledger rows have no scenario bound to them: `foldInsert_inversion`, `update_inversion`, `update_payload_free`, `update_keeps_registry`, `bookTerminate_keeps_locked`, `release_is_terminal_fold` and `fold_signers_unchanged`. The lifecycle witness exercises most of them without being bound to them.
- The corpus exercises one controller, keys 5 and 6 and fixed amounts. It exhibits the statements and does not cover them.
- The replay check compares recomputed runs with the recorded ones. No control has yet shown that it fails on a mutated corpus.
- On-chain questions stay later-phase work: decoding cost, selected-versus-unrelated fold rows, and burn-source binding in the compiled scripts.
